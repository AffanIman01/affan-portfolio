Robot Basketball, 2025.

Your first possession

  1. Drive with WASD or the on-screen arrows.
  2. Advance across halfway. Guided dribbling performs the required bounce on the attacking side.
  3. Press E to pass. Keep at least a 1 m gap between the robots.
  4. Use Space to shoot, or approach the rim with a dunker. Aim assist calculates a ballistic trajectory.
  5. Press Q to switch teammates. Loose balls are collected when a robot reaches them.

Points & timing

2 points inside the arc; 3 when the whole bumper remains outside it; 7 for an airborne dunk. Matches use 120 or 160 seconds, a 20-second shot clock, and an 8-second advance limit.

A shot released before the buzzer may finish. Offensive rebounds keep the clock; defensive rebounds reset possession. A stationary catch-and-shoot is allowed for a normal shot. A dunk needs a dribble.

What is modeled

Official court dimensions, half-court advance, pass spacing, backcourt violations, dribbling eligibility, shot clocks, scoring, restricted defense, rebounds and possession changes. Bumper contact is permitted; pushing is prevented by collision constraints.

Model limits

The four mechanisms are original concepts, not replicas of competing teams. Ball flight uses gravity, simplified rim/backboard impacts and bouncing. Wheel grip, motor torque, spin/Magnus forces, structural loads, detailed foul adjudication and physical feasibility are not validated.

Practice removes time pressure and opposing defense. Referee setup and possession changes are shortened. Ties use fewer successful baskets, then fastest successful possession; unresolved ties remain draws here.
